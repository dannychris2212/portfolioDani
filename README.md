I'm a product builder: product owner mindset, builder hands. I came up through customer support and operations, then started shipping products of my own. This site is how I show that work, laid out the way I think about it.

---

## How it works

- **The cover is a project chooser.** Like opening a DAW, you start by picking a session. Each card shows the result before you click.
- **Each project is an arrangement.** Tracks are workstreams (Problem, Field app, AI on WhatsApp, Onboarding…), the timeline is the project's phases (Discover → Build → Launch → Iterate → Next), and each region opens its notes in the inspector.
- **DEMO regions play screen recordings** of the real product, recorded on a mock tenant.
- **The Career session is a timeline from 2019 to now.** The music track runs underneath everything. Press play and one of my songs runs while the playhead walks through the years.

## 📝 Liner notes: the decisions behind it

### 1. The music is the frame, the product is the content
The first version led with the music, and a recruiter skimming for ten seconds would have read "musician who codes". I reordered everything so products come first and music sits underneath as the bass line. Roughly 80/20.

### 2. Proof before the click
Every card and every session header shows a result up front: a SaaS in production, a live demo, a published benchmark. Nobody should need to click to find out whether a project is real.

### 3. Problem → thesis → features
Each session opens on the Problem track and then states a thesis. Every other track follows from that thesis. That's how I build products, so it's how I present them.

### 4. One principle, two contexts
"AI first, human closer" shows up in both the field-sales CRM and the voice agents. It's the closest thing I have to a product philosophy for AI.

### 5. Privacy by default
Demos run on a fictional company with fake data, isolated from real customers. Anything personal in the recordings was cut out rather than blurred, so no single frame can leak it.

### 6. No framework, no build step
One HTML file, vanilla JS, and a folder of media. It deploys anywhere static files do, and there's nothing to update in a year.
